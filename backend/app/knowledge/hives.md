# Hives (urticaria)

## Overview
Hives are raised, itchy welts on the skin. Individual welts usually fade within 24 hours but new ones can appear. Hives lasting less than six weeks are called acute; hives that keep returning for more than six weeks are called chronic.

## Symptoms
Hives appear as raised, red or skin-coloured bumps or patches (wheals) that itch, sometimes sting, and can change shape or move around the body. Deeper swelling of the lips, eyelids, hands or feet is called angioedema.

## Causes and triggers
Common triggers include foods (such as nuts, shellfish and eggs), medicines (such as some antibiotics and painkillers), infections, insect stings, heat, cold, pressure on the skin, exercise and stress. Often no clear cause is found, especially in chronic hives.

## Treatment
Non-drowsy antihistamines are commonly used to relieve itching; a pharmacist or doctor can advise which is suitable. Avoid known triggers. Chronic hives may need specialist treatment.

## Emergency warning signs
Seek emergency care immediately if hives come with swelling of the lips, tongue or throat, difficulty breathing or swallowing, wheezing, dizziness or fainting. These can be signs of anaphylaxis, a life-threatening allergic reaction. Use an adrenaline (epinephrine) auto-injector if one has been prescribed, and call emergency services.

## When to see a doctor
See a doctor if hives keep coming back, last more than a few days, or if you are unsure what triggered them.
