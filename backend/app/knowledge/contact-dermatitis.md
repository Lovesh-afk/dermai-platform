# Contact dermatitis

## Overview
Contact dermatitis is an itchy rash caused by something touching the skin. Irritant contact dermatitis comes from substances that damage the skin, such as soaps, detergents, solvents or frequent hand washing. Allergic contact dermatitis is an immune reaction to a specific substance.

## Symptoms
It causes a red or darker, itchy rash where the substance touched the skin, sometimes with dryness, cracking, swelling, bumps or fluid-filled blisters. Allergic reactions may appear hours to a few days after contact.

## Common triggers
Common allergens include nickel in jewellery and belt buckles, fragrances, preservatives in cosmetics, hair dye (PPD), rubber and latex, some topical medicines, and plants such as poison ivy. Common irritants include detergents, cleaning products and frequent wet work.

## Treatment and self-care
Identify and avoid the trigger, wash the area gently after exposure, use cool compresses and fragrance-free moisturisers, and protect hands with suitable gloves. A pharmacist or doctor can advise on anti-itch or anti-inflammatory creams. A dermatologist can do patch testing to find the exact allergen.

## When to see a doctor
See a doctor if the rash is on the face, eyes or genitals, is widespread or severe, does not improve after avoiding the suspected trigger, or shows signs of infection such as pus, crusting or spreading painful redness.
