# Acne

## Overview
Acne is a very common skin condition in which hair follicles (pores) become clogged with oil (sebum) and dead skin cells. It usually appears on the face, forehead, chest, upper back and shoulders. It is most common in teenagers but can continue into or start in adulthood.

## Symptoms
Acne can show up as whiteheads and blackheads (clogged pores), small red tender bumps (papules), pimples with pus at their tips (pustules), and in more severe acne, large painful lumps under the skin (nodules and cysts). Severe acne can leave scars or dark marks.

## Causes and triggers
Acne develops when excess oil, dead skin cells and bacteria (Cutibacterium acnes) block pores and cause inflammation. Hormonal changes during puberty, menstrual cycles, pregnancy and some hormonal conditions can make it worse. Other factors include some medications, heavy or oily skincare and cosmetics, friction or pressure on the skin, and stress. Squeezing or picking spots makes inflammation and scarring more likely.

## Treatment options
Mild acne is often managed with over-the-counter products containing benzoyl peroxide, salicylic acid or adapalene, used as the label directs; improvement usually takes several weeks. A doctor may prescribe stronger topical treatments, antibiotics, hormonal therapy or isotretinoin for moderate to severe acne. Do not combine many strong products at once without advice, as this can irritate the skin.

## Self-care and prevention
Wash the face gently twice a day with a mild cleanser, use non-comedogenic (non-pore-clogging) moisturisers, sunscreen and makeup, and avoid picking or squeezing spots. Change pillowcases regularly and clean phone screens.

## When to see a doctor
See a dermatologist if acne is painful or cystic, is leaving scars or dark marks, is affecting your confidence or mood, or has not improved after about two to three months of over-the-counter treatment.
