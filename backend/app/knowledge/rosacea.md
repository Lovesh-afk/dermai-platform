# Rosacea

## Overview
Rosacea is a long-term condition that mainly affects the central face, causing redness and sometimes pimple-like bumps. It is most common in adults over 30. It is not contagious and is not caused by poor hygiene.

## Symptoms
Signs include persistent redness across the cheeks, nose, chin and forehead, easy flushing, visible small blood vessels, red bumps or pus-filled spots, a burning or stinging feeling, and sometimes thickened skin on the nose. Some people have dry, irritated or gritty eyes (ocular rosacea). Rosacea can look like acne but usually has no blackheads.

## Triggers
Common triggers include sun exposure, heat, hot drinks, spicy food, alcohol, strong winds or cold, intense exercise, stress, and some skincare products.

## Treatment and self-care
A doctor may prescribe creams or gels such as metronidazole, azelaic acid or ivermectin, or oral medicines for bumps; laser treatment can reduce visible vessels. Daily broad-spectrum sunscreen, gentle fragrance-free skincare and tracking and avoiding personal triggers all help.

## When to see a doctor
See a dermatologist if facial redness persists, if bumps or burning are bothering you, or if your eyes feel irritated, as untreated rosacea can gradually worsen.
