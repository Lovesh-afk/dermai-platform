# Fungal skin infections (ringworm, athlete's foot, jock itch)

## Overview
Fungal skin infections are caused by fungi that thrive in warm, moist areas. Common types are ringworm on the body (tinea corporis), athlete's foot (tinea pedis), jock itch (tinea cruris), scalp ringworm (tinea capitis), nail infections (onychomycosis), tinea versicolor, and yeast (candida) infections in skin folds. Despite the name, ringworm is not caused by a worm.

## Symptoms
Ringworm typically looks like a red, scaly, itchy ring-shaped patch with a clearer centre that slowly spreads outward. Athlete's foot causes itching, peeling and cracking between the toes. Jock itch causes an itchy red rash in the groin and inner thighs. Tinea versicolor causes lighter or darker discoloured patches, often on the chest and back. Nail infections make nails thick, discoloured and brittle.

## How it spreads
Many fungal infections are contagious. They spread through direct skin contact, from infected pets, and by sharing towels, clothing, combs or shoes, or walking barefoot in damp shared areas such as locker rooms and pool sides. Sweating, tight clothing, diabetes and a weakened immune system increase the risk.

## Treatment options
Many body, foot and groin infections respond to over-the-counter antifungal creams such as clotrimazole, miconazole or terbinafine, used for as long as the product label directs, which often includes continuing for a while after the rash clears. Scalp and nail infections usually need prescription oral antifungal medicine. Steroid creams used alone can make a fungal infection worse and harder to diagnose, so avoid them unless a doctor advises otherwise.

## Prevention
Keep skin clean and dry, especially between the toes and in skin folds; wear breathable clothing and change socks and underwear daily; do not share towels, combs or shoes; wear sandals in shared showers; and have pets with patchy hair loss checked by a vet.

## When to see a doctor
See a doctor if the infection is on the scalp or nails, keeps spreading, has not improved after about two weeks of over-the-counter treatment, keeps coming back, or if you have diabetes or a weakened immune system.
