# Eczema (atopic dermatitis)

## Overview
Eczema, also called atopic dermatitis, is a long-term condition that makes the skin dry, itchy and inflamed. It often starts in childhood but can affect people of any age. It tends to come and go in flare-ups. Eczema is not contagious.

## Symptoms
Common signs are dry, very itchy skin and red or darker patches, often in the creases of the elbows and knees, on the hands, neck and face. Skin may become rough, thickened, cracked or weepy during flares. Scratching can worsen the rash and disturb sleep.

## Causes and triggers
Eczema involves a weakened skin barrier and an overactive immune response, and it often runs in families with asthma or hay fever. Common triggers include harsh soaps, fragrances, wool and some fabrics, sweat, heat, dry weather, stress, dust mites, pet dander and pollen.

## Treatment options
The foundation of care is moisturising frequently with fragrance-free emollients, especially right after bathing, and using gentle soap-free cleansers. Flares are commonly treated with anti-inflammatory creams such as topical corticosteroids or calcineurin inhibitors, which a doctor or pharmacist can advise on. More severe eczema may need light therapy or newer prescription medicines.

## Self-care and prevention
Take short lukewarm baths or showers, pat the skin dry, moisturise straight away, wear soft breathable fabrics such as cotton, keep nails short to limit scratch damage, and identify and avoid your personal triggers.

## When to see a doctor
See a doctor if eczema is widespread, keeps you awake, is not improving with regular moisturising, or shows signs of infection such as yellow crusts, pus, spreading redness, or painful clustered blisters.
