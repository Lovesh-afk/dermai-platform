# Psoriasis

## Overview
Psoriasis is a long-term immune-related condition that speeds up skin cell turnover, causing thick patches of skin. It is not contagious. It often comes in cycles of flares and calmer periods.

## Symptoms
Typical signs are raised, well-defined red or darker patches (plaques) covered with silvery-white scale, commonly on the elbows, knees, scalp and lower back. Patches can itch, burn or crack. Nails may show pitting, thickening or separation. Some people develop joint pain and stiffness, known as psoriatic arthritis.

## Causes and triggers
Psoriasis results from an overactive immune response combined with genetic factors. Flares can be triggered by stress, infections such as strep throat, skin injury, cold dry weather, smoking, heavy alcohol use and some medications.

## Treatment options
Treatment depends on severity. Options include moisturisers, topical corticosteroids, vitamin D analogue creams, coal tar or salicylic acid products, light therapy (phototherapy), and for moderate to severe disease, prescription tablets or injectable biologic medicines.

## When to see a doctor
See a dermatologist to confirm the diagnosis and plan treatment, especially if patches are widespread, painful, affecting daily life, or if you notice joint pain, swelling or stiffness.
