# Skin cancer and changing moles

## Overview
Skin cancer is the abnormal growth of skin cells, mostly caused by ultraviolet (UV) damage from the sun or tanning beds. The main types are basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma and melanoma. Melanoma is less common but more dangerous. Most skin cancers are highly treatable when found early.

## Warning signs of melanoma (ABCDE)
Check moles using ABCDE: Asymmetry (one half differs from the other), Border (irregular, ragged or blurred edges), Colour (several shades or uneven colour), Diameter (larger than about 6 mm, though melanomas can be smaller), and Evolving (any change in size, shape, colour, height, or new itching or bleeding). A mole that looks different from your other moles (the "ugly duckling" sign) also deserves a check.

## Other warning signs
Basal cell carcinoma often looks like a pearly or waxy bump, or a sore that bleeds, heals and returns. Squamous cell carcinoma may appear as a firm red nodule or a scaly, crusted patch. Any spot or sore that does not heal within a few weeks should be checked.

## Risk factors
Risk is higher with frequent sun exposure, a history of sunburns (especially in childhood), tanning bed use, fair skin that burns easily, many or unusual moles, a personal or family history of skin cancer, and a weakened immune system. People with darker skin can still get skin cancer, including on the palms, soles and under nails.

## When to see a doctor
See a dermatologist promptly for any new, changing, bleeding, itchy or non-healing spot, or any mole showing ABCDE warning signs. Early checks save lives; do not wait for a spot to become painful.
